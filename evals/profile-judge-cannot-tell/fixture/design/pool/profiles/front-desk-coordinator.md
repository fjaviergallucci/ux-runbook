# Front-desk coordinator

The person who runs the front desk of a small clinic. Nobody of this profession has been heard yet: every characteristic below is a guess, written from what the founder said at setup, but one. Its usage context is unknown: nobody has said what she uses the product on.

A profile is what is true of a profession, the same from one surface to the next. Every characteristic
has four fields, and each field is a block of its own: it begins with its label and ends at the next
blank line. The first three are the traits. The fourth is what a profile judge holds a design to, and a
trait without one is trivia. An audience is handed the traits and never the fourth field, which is
taken out by that rule: so every label stays as it is written, at the start of its line.

No trait is invented: what nobody has said is a guess or unknown, and stays so until it has a source.
The card never speaks for a country, a specialty or a setting outside its scope. When an audience gets
stuck where no fourth field predicted, a characteristic is missing here, and it is added.

## Scope

What is true: The coordinators of the front desk of a clinic of up to twelve rooms, with one or two people at the desk, in one country. Not the practitioners, and not a hospital's admissions desk.

Who it does not hold for: A clinic with a call centre, where whoever answers the phone never sees the waiting room.

Evidence: guess

So the interface must: Never count on a second person at the desk to take the phone or the queue.

## The work

What is true: They put each patient in a room with a practitioner: those who booked, and those who walk in. Done is a patient walking to a room that is really free.

Who it does not hold for: A coordinator who only takes payments.

Evidence: guess

So the interface must: Say what is free now before anything else. Call a room by the name on its door.

## Technological familiarity

What is true: They use a booking calendar, a messaging app and a spreadsheet every day, and learned each by doing. They do not read a manual.

Who it does not hold for: The coordinator in her first week.

Evidence: guess

So the interface must: Need no pattern those three tools do not have. Use no software word where the clinic has its own.

## Usage context

What is true: She works at the clinic's front desk, with people waiting in front of her. What she uses the product on is not known: a computer on the desk with a keyboard, a tablet on a stand or a phone in her hand. Nobody has said, and nobody has been asked.

Who it does not hold for: Not known yet.

Evidence: unknown

So the interface must: Not known yet.

## Time and attention

What is true: She is interrupted every few minutes: the phone, the door, a practitioner with a question. She gives the screen a glance, many times an hour, all day.

Who it does not hold for: The coordinator on the early shift, before the doors open.

Evidence: guess

So the interface must: Let the main job be done in two presses from the first screen. Keep what she had started when she looks back after an interruption.

## Error tolerance and stakes

What is true: A patient sent to a room that is not free walks in on someone else's treatment. Before she sends anyone, she checks that the room is free now, and until when.

Who it does not hold for: Nobody at a front desk.

Evidence: guess

So the interface must: Show, for a free room, until when it is free, beside the action that books it. Let a booking made in the wrong room be taken back at once.

## Domain vocabulary

What is true: A room, by the name on its door. A walk-in. A booking. A practitioner, by first name. A room is "free", "busy", "being cleaned" or "closed". They never say "resource", "slot" or "asset".

Who it does not hold for: A clinic that numbers its rooms and never names them.

Evidence: guess

So the interface must: Use those words, and none of the three they never say.

## Specialties

One `###` section for each specialty, named as a person's card names it in `specialty`. Under it, only
the characteristics it overrides or adds, each under a `####` heading with its four fields. Whatever a
specialty does not mention is inherited from the base.

None yet: nobody has described a specialty of this profession.

## Optional characteristics

A characteristic is defined only once it has a section of its own, with the four fields. Those listed
here are not defined yet. When a design touches one, it is added as a `##` section above "Specialties",
under the name given here, and its line here is deleted.

- **Confidentiality and regulation** — what must stay private, and the rules that bind them.
- **Delegation and hierarchy** — an assistant, a trainee, who answers for the work, who pays.
- **Physical conditions** — gloves, a dim room, eyesight.
- **Tools it must fit beside** — what it takes from them, and what it hands back.
- **What earns and what loses their trust** — what they look for before they rely on a tool.
- **How they learn a tool** — alone, from a colleague, from a course.
- **Country variation** — the names, the formats and the documents that differ from one country to the next.
- **The rhythm of the work** — shifts, batches at the end of the day, where they can stop and resume.
