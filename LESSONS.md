# Lessons · the kit

Appended, never rewritten: an entry that proved wrong is answered by a later one. One dated entry for
each lesson, and only what is true of the process on any project: nothing of one product, one person or
one profession. What one design taught goes in that project's own `LESSONS.md`.

An entry has the shape `skills/design/references/lessons.md` gives a project's, less what belongs to one
design: its heading is the day and the lesson, and under it what was tried, what happened, and what the
next design does differently. The conductor proposes an entry; it lands here by an ordinary change to
this repository.

The first eleven entries of 2026-11-15 are the seed: what the two processes this pipeline was adapted
from had already learned, each said so that it holds anywhere. What follows them was learned by this
pipeline itself.

## 2026-11-15 · A brief that prescribes the design returns the session's own idea, in several voices

**What was tried.** One brief sent to several makers, written by the session that already had an answer
in mind, and naming it: the parts, their arrangement, an example "for instance".

**What happened.** Every proposal was that answer, reworded: as many voices as there were makers, and
one idea. Range came back only when the brief said who it was for, what she had to get done and what
the product could do, and nothing else.

**What the next design does differently.** A brief gives context, never the design. It is read sentence
by sentence before it goes out: a sentence a maker could draw a screen from is cut, or turned into a
fact. The founder reads it first.

## 2026-11-15 · Nothing is shortlisted, detailed or drawn before the founder marks

**What was tried.** Under a deadline, the session chose a few of the proposals itself, "for speed", and
had them made finished before he had seen any of them.

**What happened.** All of it was set aside. The pick had been the session's and not his, and the
finished work was paid for twice.

**What the next design does differently.** Every valid proposal reaches him, cheap and unfinished, and
nothing is shortlisted, detailed or drawn before he has marked. A deadline shortens the number of
rounds, never the order of the gates.

## 2026-11-15 · His own sentences are the calibration for words

**What was tried.** Words written from a description of the voice: a register, a list of qualities, a
style to follow.

**What happened.** The lines came back correct and sounded like nobody. One or two sentences he had
said himself, handed over as he said them, moved the writing further than any description of a voice.

**What the next design does differently.** When the words will matter, he is asked for one or two
sentences as he would say them, before anything is written. They go to the writer's brief word for
word, as the bar its lines must meet.

## 2026-11-15 · A number named to him is counted by a tool first

**What was tried.** Totals written from memory or by eye: how many items, how many files, how many
strings in each language.

**What happened.** Some were wrong, and one wrong count made him doubt the rest of the page it was on.

**What the next design does differently.** A number in an artefact, on a decision page or in a message
to him is counted by a command first, and comes from what the command printed.

## 2026-11-15 · Options that are one idea in three palettes are not a choice

**What was tried.** Several options put in front of him that shared one structure and differed in
finish: the colour, the type, the mood.

**What happened.** He was asked to choose and had nothing to choose between. The option that looked
most finished won, and what it locked in had never been compared with anything.

**What the next design does differently.** Options differ in kind. Structures are described, then drawn
by one script in one plain kit, so that they differ in structure and in nothing else. Structure is
chosen before look.

## 2026-11-15 · A direction given in adjectives goes many rounds; one given in references and refused tells converges

**What was tried.** A visual direction asked for in adjectives, the same few words every round.

**What happened.** Round followed round: each maker read the words its own way, and each result fitted
them. It converged when the direction was given as things to point at, with a list of the tells it
refused.

**What the next design does differently.** A direction is described by references and by what it must
not become, each tell named. An adjective is never a brief.

## 2026-11-15 · A simulated reader agrees too easily: only the ranking carries information

**What was tried.** A simulated audience asked, option by option, whether it understood and whether it
would go on.

**What happened.** It said yes to nearly everything, and understood what it had been told to
understand. Its yeses told nothing. What told the options apart was the order it put them in, and the
places where it got stuck.

**What the next design does differently.** Every simulated read opens with a caveat that says so. Only
its ranking and its stuck points go to the decision page. A real person's read, whenever there is one,
takes the seat and outranks it.

## 2026-11-15 · Safe briefs and judges who reward reduction make work cleaner and emptier

**What was tried.** A brief written to be consistent, and blind judges who scored every round.

**What happened.** The score rose each round and the work got plainer each round. At its highest score
he found it too simple: nothing had been taken out that was wrong, and nothing was left that he
recognised.

**What the next design does differently.** A judge answers the job before the craft: does this do what
it is for, for the person it is for? A brief asks for range. Nothing is scored, and a ledger names what
is missing as well as what is in the way.

## 2026-11-15 · One artefact for each review, and one question

**What was tried.** Several things put to him in one approval: a batch of screens, a sheet of many
variants, a long page with several questions in it.

**What happened.** One yes covered what nobody had looked at. The faults in it were found later, when
they cost more.

**What the next design does differently.** One artefact for each review, and one question about one
thing. The next thing starts after his answer.

## 2026-11-15 · A stage skipped is the founder's decision, and it is logged

**What was tried.** Stages the session judged unnecessary were left out, without a word.

**What happened.** The later stages inherited the gap, and it surfaced late, in finished work, as a
defect nobody could trace.

**What the next design does differently.** A stage is run, or the route says why it is not. Only the
founder skips one, and the state keeps his reason.

## 2026-11-15 · Record whose proposals are picked, and let that decide which seats stay

**What was tried.** The same makers and judges seated on every piece, by habit, each costing an agent
that reads its inputs.

**What happened.** Nobody could say which of them had ever changed a decision, or whose proposals he
picked, so none could be dropped and none could be defended.

**What the next design does differently.** Each design's lessons record which seats' findings changed a
decision, whose proposal was picked, and the model each ran on. A seat stays, goes, or changes model on
that record, never on habit.

## 2026-11-15 · His words are asked for once, at every design: "when the words will matter" was no test

**What was tried.** The entry above on his own sentences, taken as a rule: he was asked for them "when
the words will matter", and the session that conducts was left to say when that was.

**What happened.** On a run of the whole pipeline the test could not be applied without judging the
design: whether the words of a surface will matter is known only once it is designed. So the question
was not asked, and the words he uses for the things on the screen, and the one he never wants her to
read, reached no brief.

**What the next design does differently.** His words are asked for once, at every design, where the
facts are gathered and before anyone has an idea: what she calls these things, and any word she must
never see. "None" is an answer, and is written as one. What he says still goes to the writer's brief
word for word, as the bar its lines must meet: that part of the earlier entry stands.
