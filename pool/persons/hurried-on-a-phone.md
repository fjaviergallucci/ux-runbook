# Hurried, on a phone

An archetype, not someone anyone met: every line on this card is a guess.
She stands for whoever uses a product away from the desk, with one hand and half her attention. Her scene
is set in the kit's example product, a room-booking tool at a small clinic's front desk: in another
product, keep her and change the setting.

## The moment

Jo is walking a patient down the corridor of a small clinic to Room 3, his coat and a folder over her
left arm, when the phone in her pocket buzzes. A colleague has finished early and wants to know whether
the garden room is free at eleven. The desk and its big screen are round the corner. She takes the phone
out with her right hand and opens the room-booking tool with her thumb, still walking, the phone at arm's
length and the light from a window across its screen. She has about fifteen seconds before they reach the
door. Halfway through, the patient will ask her something, and by the time she looks back the phone may
have locked.

## The job

Find out whether one room is free at one time, and book it, with one thumb, in the length of a corridor.
And after the interruption, carry on from where she was, not from the start. Done, to her, is being able
to answer "yes, it's yours" and put the phone back in her pocket.

## What she never does

- Use her other hand. What sits in the far top corner of the screen is out of her thumb's reach, and she
  does not pinch, zoom or turn the phone.
- Type more than a word. She picks from what is offered, and a form with five fields waits until she is
  back at the desk.
- Try a third time. What she misses twice while walking, she gives up on.
- Read a paragraph. She reads a line, in a glance, at arm's length.
- Watch a screen that is still loading for more than a few steps. She pockets the phone without knowing
  whether it worked.
- Start again. If the screen has gone back to the beginning when she looks at it next, the booking waits
  until she is at the desk, and by then someone else may have the room.

## The check

With one thumb, a glance at a time and an interruption in the middle, can she finish, and does the screen
show her where she was when she looks back?
