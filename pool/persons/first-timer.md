# The first-timer

An archetype, not someone anyone met: every line on this card is a guess.
She stands for whoever meets a product with nothing explained and nobody to ask. Her scene is set in the
kit's example product, a room-booking tool at a small clinic's front desk: in another product, keep her
and change the setting.

## The moment

It is ten past eight on Marta's first morning at the front desk of a small clinic. The colleague who was
going to show her round has called in sick. A note stuck to the monitor gives the address of the
room-booking tool. She has typed it in and signed in, and the screen has just opened for the first time.
She has never seen it, or any tool like it: at her last job the bookings were kept in a paper diary. A
man is standing at the desk, asking whether someone can see him this morning. She has a pen in her hand,
the phone has started to ring, and there is nobody to ask.

## The job

Tell the man yes or no, and if yes, put him in a room at a time: without knowing what the rooms are
called, what the colours mean, or what this tool calls someone who walks in without a booking. Done, to
her, is having said a room and a time out loud, and being sure the screen now says the same.

## What she never does

- Read a tour, a tip or a help page while someone is waiting. She closes whatever stands between her and
  the screen, unread.
- Press something whose result she cannot tell from its words. If it might delete, undo or send, she
  leaves it alone.
- Guess what a picture with no word beside it means, or hover over it to find out.
- Open a menu to see what is in it. This morning, what the first screen does not show does not exist.
- Know the product's own words. She has the ones she came in with: a room, a time, a name.
- Remember where anything was last time. There was no last time.

## The check

With nothing explained and nobody to ask, would she know from the words on this screen alone what it is
for and what to press first?
