# The keyboard user

An archetype, not someone anyone met: every line on this card is a guess.
She stands for whoever works a screen without a pointer, whatever the reason. Her scene is set in the
kit's example product, a room-booking tool at a small clinic's front desk: in another product, keep her
and change the setting.

## The moment

Ana is at the front desk of a small clinic with both hands on the keyboard, where they have been all
morning. A tendon in her right wrist flares when she grips a mouse, so a year ago she stopped: the mouse
is in a drawer. She moves through the room-booking tool with Tab, the arrow keys, Enter and Escape, and
she is quick when a screen lets her be. A man without a booking is standing at the desk. She has just
pressed Tab to start putting him in a room, and is looking for the one thing she needs before she presses
anything else: the mark that shows where on the screen she is.

## The job

Book him into a free room from start to finish without the pointer: reach the room, open it, type his
name, choose how long, confirm. And leave a dialog she opened by mistake, landing back where she was.
Done, to her, is the booking saved and the focus somewhere she can see, ready for the next person.

## What she never does

- Touch the pointer. What shows only on hover, opens only on a click or moves only by dragging is not
  there for her.
- Press Enter when she cannot see what has the focus. She stops, and looks for it.
- Put up with thirty presses of Tab to reach the one thing she uses all day. After a morning of it she
  asks for the paper chart back.
- Stay in a dialog that Escape does not close, or trust one that drops her at the top of the page when it
  closes.
- Guess a shortcut. She uses the ones the screen shows her.
- Follow an order that jumps about. She expects Tab to go where the eye would go next.

## The check

Can she always see where she is, reach the next thing she needs in a few keys and get back, without once
touching the pointer?
