# The second-language reader

An archetype, not someone anyone met: every line on this card is a guess.
She stands for whoever reads an interface in a language that is not her first. Her scene is set in the
kit's example product, a room-booking tool at a small clinic's front desk: in another product, keep her
and change the setting.

## The moment

Ana has worked the front desk of a small clinic for two months. The language on the screen is her second.
She speaks it with patients all day without trouble. Reading it is slower, word by word, and she reads
every word, because skimming is the one thing she cannot yet do in it. It is the quiet half hour after
lunch, and the waiting room is empty. A patient has written to ask whether tomorrow's appointment can
move to the afternoon. Ana has tried to move it, and the room-booking tool has answered with a message:
three short lines about Room 1, with two buttons under them. Her hands are on the keyboard, and her
phone, with its translation app, lies beside it. She has all the time she needs, and nobody is watching.
But she is about to write back to the patient, and she will not write a word until she is sure what the
message says has already happened, and what will happen if she presses.

## The job

Work out from the message what has already been done to the booking and what each button would do, choose
one, and then tell the patient in her own words when to come. Done, to her, is having sent that answer
and knowing it is true, not hoping so.

## What she never does

- Skim. She reads every word in order, so every extra word costs her, and a long sentence loses her
  before its verb.
- Guess at an idiom, a joke or an abbreviation: in English, "You're all set", "Heads up", "N/A". She
  takes each word at its dictionary meaning, and stops when that makes no sense.
- Fill in what a clipped line leaves out. "Booking moved" may be telling her that it is done, or telling
  her to do it, and she cannot tell which.
- Take two words for one thing. If the button says "Book" and the message says "Reserve", she looks for
  the difference between them.
- Take anything from tone. A friendly aside or an exclamation mark is more words to get through.
- Put a whole sentence through the translation app on her phone. She looks up one word at a time, and a
  word the product made up, or a label cut short, comes back as nonsense.

## The check

Read word by word, each word at its dictionary meaning, would this screen tell her what has already
happened and what each button will do?
