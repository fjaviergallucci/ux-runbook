# {The profession}

{A line or two: the profession this card speaks for, and how much of it is known and how much guessed.}

A profile is what is true of a profession, the same from one surface to the next. Every characteristic
has four fields, and each field is a block of its own: it begins with its label and ends at the next
blank line. The first three are the traits. The fourth is what a profile judge holds a design to, and a
trait without one is trivia. An audience is handed the traits and never the fourth field, which is
taken out by that rule: so every label stays as it is written, at the start of its line.

No trait is invented: what nobody has said is a guess or unknown, and stays so until it has a source.
What only the founder said of the profession is a guess too, written `guess (the founder, <date>)`
under "Evidence", until someone of that profession has been heard.
The card never speaks for a country, a specialty or a setting outside its scope. When an audience gets
stuck where no fourth field predicted, a characteristic is missing here, and it is added.

## Specialties

One `###` section for each specialty, named as a person's card names it in `specialty`. Under it, only
the characteristics it overrides or adds, each under a `####` heading with its four fields. Whatever a
specialty does not mention is inherited from the base.

## Optional characteristics

A characteristic is defined only once it has a section of its own, with the four fields. Those listed
here are not defined yet. When a design touches one, it is added as a `##` section above "Specialties",
under the name given here, and its line here is deleted.

- **Confidentiality and regulation** — what must stay private, and the rules that bind them.
- **Delegation and hierarchy** — an assistant, a trainee, who answers for the work, who pays.
- **Physical conditions** — gloves, a dim room, eyesight.
- **Tools it must fit beside** — what it takes from them, and what it hands back.
- **What earns and what loses their trust** — what they look for before they rely on a tool.
- **How they learn a tool** — alone, from a colleague, from a course.
- **Country variation** — the names, the formats and the documents that differ from one country to the next.
- **The rhythm of the work** — shifts, batches at the end of the day, where they can stop and resume.
