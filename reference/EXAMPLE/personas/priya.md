# Priya — front desk, physiotherapy clinic

**Context.** Standing at a desk in a small reception area, on a 24-inch monitor angled away from
her, frequently holding a phone to one ear. There is often someone waiting in front of her. Her
attention on this screen is measured in seconds, not minutes, and it is never her only task.

**Daily workflow.** She opens RoomBoard when she arrives and never closes it. Through the day she
answers the same question over and over — a walk-in arrives, or a practitioner finishes early, or
someone calls to move an appointment — and each time she needs to know what is free right now and
put a name in it. At the end of the day she checks nothing has been left unmarked for cleaning.

**Frustration.** The last product they tried showed her the whole week when she only ever cares
about the next twenty minutes. She abandoned it after three weeks and went back to the laminated
wall chart, which she can read from four metres away while on the phone — and which is, by her own
account, better at the only thing she needs.

**Primary goal.** Know what is free right now. Everything else on the screen is secondary to that
one question, which she asks perhaps forty times a day.

**Tech comfort.** Moderate. Fluent with the tools she uses daily; no patience for learning a new one
and no time to.

**Frequency.** Constantly. The screen is open all day and glanced at dozens of times.

**Never does.** Looks at next week. Runs a report. Changes a setting. Adjusts her own view. She has
never once used a filter in any product the clinic has bought.

**Surfaces.** `today` (primary, all day), `settings` (twice ever).

---

## The quality check

**What does this persona tell me to do that I wouldn't have done anyway?**

Three things, none of which were in the original brief:

1. **Don't build a calendar.** A calendar answers "what is the shape of the week." She asks "what is
   free in the next twenty minutes," forty times a day. Those are different products, and the
   original specification was for the wrong one.

2. **Don't offer a filter — do the filtering.** She has never used a filter. A "show free rooms"
   control would be a control she never touches, occupying the space where the answer should be.
   Free rooms get their own leading region; the screen does the work.

3. **Optimise for a glance from an angle, not for a reading.** The laminated chart is legible at
   four metres. That sets the bar for type size, contrast, and how much can be on screen at once —
   and it rules out the comfortable, information-dense default that would otherwise have been built.
